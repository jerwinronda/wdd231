Oroquieta City

Oroquieta City, officially the City of Oroquieta, is a coastal component city and the capital of Misamis Occidental province in the Northern Mindanao region of the Philippines. It has a population of 72,301 as of the 2020 census and covers an area of 237.88 square kilometers. The city was originally known as Layawan, named after the Layawan River, and was granted township status in 1884. It became the provincial capital in 1930. Oroquieta is known for its hospitality, agriculture, and eco-cultural tourism. The city's name is believed to be derived from the Spanish word "oro" (gold) and "quita" (to take away), hinting at its past as a gold-rich area. The city's development has attracted migrants from neighboring areas, fostering growth through fishing, farming, and trade.
